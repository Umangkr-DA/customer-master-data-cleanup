# Business impact estimate (illustrative)

Generated by `python scripts/run_phase6.py`. **Every number below is computed.** The *assumptions* are in
[`config/assumptions.json`](../config/assumptions.json); they are placeholders, not facts about any
company. The rates they multiply are measured on synthetic data. This shows how the pieces combine, not what
a real file would save, and the outcome flips with the unit costs (see the break-even rows).

## Result for a customer file of 1,000,000 source records

| Item | Per year |
|---|---|
| Duplicate records correctly removed | 171,241 |
| Duplicates still unmerged | 55,663 |
| Over-merged clusters (customers mixed up) | 3,286 |
| Duplicate messages avoided | 1,643,912 |
| Gross saving | INR 410,978 |
| Cost of wrong merges | INR 657,132 |
| **Net, auto-merge only** | **INR -246,154** |
| Review of 50,000 pairs: analyst cost | INR 500,000 |
| Review: extra duplicates found | 37,400 (worth INR 89,760) |
| Review: extra wrong merges | 600 (cost INR 120,000) |
| **Net, auto-merge + review** | **INR -776,394** |

## Reading it

* Auto-merge does NOT pay for itself on messaging savings alone under these assumptions. It breaks even if one wrong merge costs at most INR 125.08 (assumed: INR 200).
* Review does NOT pay for itself (simulated reviewer accuracy 95%). It would break even if removing one extra duplicate were worth INR 16.58 a year (modelled at INR 2.40).
* Not modelled, and likely larger than e-mail/SMS savings: postal mail, analytics and reporting errors from double-counted customers, compliance (one customer, one consent record), and contact reachability below.

## Where the rates come from (measured)

* Matching outcomes: held-out ledger entry of the frozen Fellegi-Sunter matcher (read from the ledger, not re-run). duplicates_removed_correct = records - predicted clusters - over-merged clusters (a lower bound on correct removals); duplicates_missed = true redundant records - correct removals.
* Review: outputs/review_simulation.csv (dev, SIMULATED reviewer, ranked queue); 250 pairs reviewed moved dev recall from 0.735 to 0.863.

## Contact reachability (dev data, measured)

| View | Share with a usable phone or e-mail |
|---|---|
| Source records, as received | 88.1% |
| Source records, standardized | 98.7% |
| Customers, any source record usable as received | 89.5% |
| Customers, golden record (standardized + survivorship) | 98.9% |

reachable = a value passing the project's strict phone (R2) or e-mail (R3) rule. The raw figures are format-strict: many raw numbers fail only because of formatting that standardization fixes, so 'raw' understates what a human could use.
